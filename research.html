Research

My research focuses on mechatronics, tactile sensing, bio-inspired sensor systems, robotics, human perception, and neurocognitive analysis in virtual reality.

Bio-Inspired Tactile Sensing

I work on whisker-like tactile sensors inspired by biological sensing systems. These sensors can help robotic systems detect contact, texture, shape, and environmental interactions.

Whisker-like sensor design concepts and fabricated prototypes from my work on modular whisker sensor design.

Sensor Calibration and Texture Analysis

My work studies how raw sensor responses can be calibrated, interpreted, and compared across different sensing mechanisms. A recurring goal is to connect sensor design, mechanical response, and task-level performance.

Calibration response used to relate magnetic flux density to bending moment at the whisker base.
Experimental setup for whisker-based surface texture analysis using a linear stage, whisker sensor, and machined surface specimen.

Robotics and Mechatronics

My robotics research includes sensor integration, automation, perception systems, and experimental platforms for intelligent physical interaction.

Whisker contact-point experiment showing sensor interaction with an obstacle.

Virtual Reality and Neurocognitive Analysis

I am also interested in studying human cognition and perception using virtual reality-based experimental systems. This includes understanding how users perceive, interact, and respond in immersive environments.
