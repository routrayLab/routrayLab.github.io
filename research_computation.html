Computation

This part of my research focuses on the computational interpretation of sensing systems. The aim is to convert raw sensor measurements into physically meaningful, task-relevant, and uncertainty-aware information for robotics, tactile perception, localization, and human-centred sensing.

Sensor Calibration and Common Representation

Image placeholder: insert a calibration plot, bending-moment mapping, or sensor-response comparison.

Sensor calibration connects raw measurements to interpretable physical quantities. For whisker-like sensors, different sensing mechanisms may produce different raw outputs, such as pressure, magnetic field variation, visual displacement, or acceleration.

A common computational goal is to map these heterogeneous outputs into a comparable representation, such as bending moment at the whisker base. This allows different sensor designs to be compared and used within the same interpretation framework.

Sensor Models and Virtual Whisker Models

Image placeholder: insert a figure showing whisker deformation, contact geometry, or virtual sensor models.

Sensor modeling is used to describe the relationship between physical contact, sensor deformation, and measured output. For whisker sensing, this involves the mechanics of a slender beam, the location of contact, the deformation shape, and the observation produced by the sensor.

A sensor model can be treated as a mapping from the state of the robot-sensor-environment system to the observation produced by the sensor.

observation = sensor model(state)
z = h(q)

Here, the state may include robot pose, whisker configuration, contact point, and environment geometry. The observation may include bending moment, contact location, deflection, slope, force, or other measurable quantities.

Preimage-Based State Estimation

Image placeholder: insert a figure showing observation preimages, feasible state sets, or state-space partitioning.

Many sensing problems are ambiguous: one observation can correspond to many possible states. Instead of forcing a single answer, preimage-based estimation represents all states that are consistent with a measurement.

preimage of observation z = all states q such that h(q) = z

This idea is useful for contact-based sensing, where a whisker observation may not uniquely determine the robot pose or contact geometry. The preimage gives a structured way to describe uncertainty and ambiguity in the sensing process.

Robot Localization

Image placeholder: insert a localization figure, robot trajectory, feasible state contraction, or map interaction.

Robot localization asks where the robot is, given sensory observations and motion information. In tactile localization, the robot may use contact observations from whisker-like sensors instead of relying only on vision or LiDAR.

The computational idea is to update the feasible set of robot states as new observations arrive. As the robot moves, each new tactile observation constrains the possible robot states. Repeated observations can reduce uncertainty and improve localization.

feasible states after new observation = previous feasible states ∩ current observation preimage

Signal Separation and Filtering

Image placeholder: insert a signal-processing figure, filtering result, or intrinsic/extrinsic response separation diagram.

Whisker sensors can respond to both external stimuli and internal platform motion. For example, a mobile robot may produce base vibrations while the whisker also senses contact, airflow, surface interaction, or terrain information.

Computational filtering is needed to separate intrinsic responses caused by robot motion from extrinsic responses caused by the environment. This supports more reliable use of whisker sensors in real robotic systems.

Trajectory Estimation for Ball Catching

Image placeholder: insert a ball trajectory prediction figure, Kinect tracking result, or catching-point estimation plot.

In robotic ball catching, computation links perception to action. The robot must detect the flying ball, estimate its trajectory, predict a feasible catching point, and move the manipulator to intercept the ball.

This problem combines visual sensing, state estimation, trajectory prediction, reachable-space reasoning, and control. It is an example of how sensing and computation can be connected to fast robotic action.

Multimodal Fusion and Texture Classification

Image placeholder: insert a VisTaNet architecture figure, texture-classification pipeline, or visual-tactile fusion diagram.

Texture perception can benefit from combining multiple sensing modalities. Visual sensing can capture global surface information, while tactile sensing can capture local interaction details.

This research direction includes tactile-only classification, visual-only classification, visual-tactile fusion, attention-guided feature fusion, and machine learning methods for roughness and hardness perception.
